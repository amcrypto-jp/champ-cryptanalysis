Subject: Technical assessment of the supplied CHAMP specification and reproducible evidence

Dear CHAMP authors,

Please find attached my technical assessment of the supplied ten-page CHAMP
Algorithm Specifications and the accompanying release CHAMP-Review-v1.0.1.zip.
The report identifies the exact reviewed files by SHA-256. It also distinguishes
your separate twelve-page preprint, including its citation of Mullan–Tsaban.

The principal issue is a subgroup collision-search strategy adapted directly to
the implemented determinant-2 pair. The package includes twelve reduced-parameter
collision pairs, complete messages and an independent verifier. The projected
full-parameter cost depends on mixing assumptions; I do not claim to have found
a practical collision at either full parameter set.

Other findings concern forgeries against naive keyed uses of CHAMP, disclosure
from related digests, a short-input meet-in-the-middle preimage search, the
determinant and length arguments, Julia/C serialization, marked statistical-test
results, and C API and initialization behavior. The keyed constructions are
assessed as possible uses, not attributed to you as proposed MAC schemes. The
report states the limits of each demonstration and does not claim a distinct
second preimage or recovery of a uniformly random long input from a single digest.

The findings, derivations and reproduction experiments were completed
independently of Markku-Juhani O. Saarinen's report at
https://ngcc.dev/reports/hash-04.html and the CryptHash Forum comments by
cuihr26 (signed "Tsinghua Hash Lab") and Cryptanalysts001 (signed "ISCAS").
I became aware of these sources on 22 September 2026, after completing this
review and its reproducibility package. The citations were added afterward to
acknowledge related work. References 9–11 give the individual source links;
the report and findings index distinguish overlap from additional contributions.

I would particularly appreciate your assessment of:

1. The subgroup construction, its mixing assumptions and implications for
   separately stated collision-security targets.
2. The intended digest encoding and whether the C/KAT convention is normative.
3. The exact scope and distribution behind the single-digest bit-prediction claim.
4. The four marked rows in the supplied statistical reports and the tested subset.
5. Intended digest-length validation, concurrent initialization and treatment of
   confidential inputs in the C implementations.

I welcome corrections and a response identifying any revised specification or
implementation files. The report is intended to support an accurate technical
discussion with you and the wider cryptography community.
